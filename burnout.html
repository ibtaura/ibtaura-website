Clarus Health: a burnout wearable that spots risk early

Burnout builds over months, and the body often carries the load before anyone names it. Clarus Health by Adarna Inc. is a health wearable for productivity and burnout: a band on your upper arm that reads your heart rhythm and six more signals, and an iPhone app that estimates when your burnout risk is rising compared with your own normal.

What burnout looks like in your body

Burnout is often described as a state of mind, but it develops when demands exceed resources for so long that the body's stress systems rarely get to switch off. None of the signs below is specific to burnout, and each can have other causes. Together, and over weeks, they form a recognisable pattern. Our article on burnout and the body covers this in more depth.

  • Heart rate variability trends. HRV, the natural variation in time between heartbeats, tends to fall as stress builds. A sustained decline against your own normal is commonly associated with accumulated stress, poor sleep, illness or overtraining. Learn more about heart rate variability.
  • Resting heart rate. When the nervous system stays on alert, resting heart rate can drift upward by a few beats per minute and stay there.
  • Sleep and recovery. Sleep is often the first casualty. It can become lighter and more broken even when the hours look adequate, and recovery may no longer rebound after a weekend.
  • Stress signals. Constant deadlines and always being reachable can keep the stress response partly engaged, and the normal daily rhythm of cortisol can become flattened or irregular. Our guide to stress resilience explains how the body learns to bounce back.

The key word is relative. HRV and resting heart rate vary widely between people, with age and with fitness, so a single number says very little. What matters is how your values move compared with your own typical range.

How Clarus Health estimates burnout risk

The band reads seven signals: heart rate, beat-to-beat timing, breathing, skin temperature, blood oxygen, steps and activity. While you sit, three soft electrodes follow your heart's rhythm, and when you move, the band switches to its optical sensor on its own. The iPhone app then compares your recent readings with your own normal over the last 14 days, rather than with an average of other people.

The Burnout Risk card

The Home screen includes a Burnout Risk card that shows how likely burnout looks right now, measured against your own baseline from the last 14 days. When the estimate climbs, you see it early and can plan a lighter day or more breaks. It is an estimate to guide your routine, not a diagnosis.

Recovery breaks that fit a working day

Spotting rising risk helps only if you can act on it. The Recovery tab in the iPhone app gathers short breaks that suit the hour, each with its length and what the evidence says:

  • Ten minutes between meetings, so stress does not pile up from one call to the next.
  • Getting outside early in the day.
  • A short nap from the Sleep option when you need to reset.

The app also offers a pause after a long stretch of stress with one tap, and you can log a break you took on your own. On the Mac, the Clarus Health Desktop app brings your capacity score, calendar and focus sessions to the computer where you work.

Short breaks are one part of recovery, and sleep is the other. Read why recovery is the missing piece in performance, and how sleep debt builds up over a busy week.

Who Clarus Health is for

Clarus Health is made for the desk. The band sits on your upper arm, clear of your keyboard and trackpad, so you can forget you are wearing it through long stretches of work. It suits people whose work combines high, sustained demand with little time to recover:

  • Knowledge workers with demanding days of meetings, deep work and deadlines.
  • Founders who carry a heavy workload with little natural separation between effort and rest.
  • Remote workers, for whom the line between home and work is easy to lose and being always reachable can feel normal.

Burnout wearable FAQ

Can a wearable detect burnout?

Not on its own. A wearable cannot diagnose burnout, whose signs overlap with depression, anxiety and several physical conditions. What it can do is show trends that are hard to notice, such as a resting heart rate creeping upward or HRV staying below your usual range. Clarus Health uses those body signals to estimate your burnout risk, not to diagnose it.

How is the burnout risk calculated?

The Clarus Health band reads your heart rhythm and six more signals through the day. The iPhone app compares your recent readings, such as heart rhythm, sleep and recovery signals, with your own normal over the last 14 days rather than with an average of other people, and shows the result on the Burnout Risk card on the Home screen.

Does it work with iPhone and Mac?

Yes. Clarus Health comes with an iPhone app, which shows your burnout risk estimate and recovery breaks, and the Clarus Health Desktop app for Mac, which brings your capacity score, calendar and focus sessions to your computer.

When does Clarus Health launch?

Clarus Health launches in July 2027.

What should I do if my burnout risk estimate is high?

Treat it as a reason to look at your sleep, workload and recovery. If exhaustion, low mood or hopelessness persist for more than a couple of weeks, or begin to affect your daily life, speak with a doctor or a qualified mental health professional, whatever any device shows.
